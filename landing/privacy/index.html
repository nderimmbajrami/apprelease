Privacy policy

AppRelease turns your Git repository into a signed release in Google Play and App Store Connect. To do that we hold a few things of yours. This page lists every one of them.

Who we are

AppRelease runs the website at apprelease.store, the console at publish.apprelease.store and the build machines that produce your releases. For anything on this page, write to support@apprelease.store. A person reads it.

What we store, and why

Your account. Name, email address and a password hash (never the password itself), and the team the account belongs to. We need these to sign you in and to tell you when a release finished or failed.

Your apps. The Git URL and branch, the Android package name and iOS bundle ID, your push rules (which branch goes to which store track) and the configuration the console generates. Without them there is nothing to build.

Your releases. Number, platform, store track, version, commit, who started it, each step's result and the build log. This is your release history, and the log is what you read when a build fails.

Your signing keys and store access. The Android upload keystore with its passwords, the Google Play service account key and the App Store Connect API key. How we protect them has its own section below.

Push events from GitHub. When you connect pushes, GitHub tells us the branch, the commit and who pushed. We use them to queue the right release and nothing else.

What we never store

Your source code. The build machine downloads it for one release and deletes the folder when the release ends, whether it succeeded, failed or was cancelled. We keep no copy.

Your app's users. AppRelease never sees the people who install your app. We build it and hand it to the stores, and that's all.

How your keys are protected

  • Encrypted before storage. Every secret is encrypted with AES in the database (OpenPGP symmetric encryption). The encryption key is not stored in the database. It exists only in the server function that saves the secret and on our build machines.
  • Unreadable from the console. The table that holds encrypted secrets has no access rule for signed-in users at all. Not even your own browser can read a secret back after you saved it. The console only shows names and public identifiers, such as the key alias or the service account email.
  • Decrypted for one build. A build machine decrypts the keys it needs when your release starts, keeps iOS certificates in a temporary keychain and deletes all of it when the release ends.
  • Kept out of logs. Passwords are replaced by *** before any line reaches the build log, and access tokens in Git URLs are hidden too.
  • Encrypted in transit. Everything between your browser, our servers, the build machines, Google and Apple goes over HTTPS.
  • Gone when you delete them. Deleting a credential, or your account, deletes the encrypted secret with it.

Found a weakness? Write to security@apprelease.store.

Who else handles your data

These services handle data on our behalf. None of them may use it for their own purposes.

  • Supabase: database, sign-in and server functions, hosted in Frankfurt, Germany (EU).
  • Hostinger: serves the website and the console.
  • Resend: sends our emails, such as the welcome mail and release notifications.
  • Google Fonts: the website loads its typefaces from Google, which sees your IP address when a page loads.

You also tell us to send data to two places: Google Play and App Store Connect receive your signed build, using your keys. And if you connect pushes, GitHub sends us push events.

Emails we send

A welcome mail when you sign up, and messages about your own releases. Our team gets a notice when an account is created and when a release is queued, with the account name, email and app, so we can make sure a build machine is ready. We send no newsletters and no marketing unless you ask for them.

Cookies and tracking

The website sets no cookies and runs no analytics or advertising. The console keeps your sign-in session and a few display preferences, such as whether you have seen the guided tour, in your browser's local storage. That's all.

What we do not do

We do not sell or rent data, we do not show ads, and we do not use your code, logs or keys to train AI models or for anything except building and publishing your apps.

How long we keep it

Apps, releases and logs stay while your account exists, so your release history is there when you need it. You can delete an app, which removes its releases and logs, or a credential at any time. Deleting your account from the console settings removes your sign-in. When you are the last member of the team, it also removes the team's apps, releases, logs and credentials.

Your rights

You can see and correct what we hold in the console, and delete it yourself as described above. For a copy of your data, or anything the console doesn't cover, write to support@apprelease.store. We answer within 30 days, usually much sooner. You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Changes

When this page changes in a way that matters to you, we tell you by email before the change takes effect.
